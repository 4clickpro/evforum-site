Charging guide

Public charging without the stress: networks, apps and etiquette

Published November 16, 2026 · EV Forum

The first time you pull up to a public charger, it can feel like arriving in a country where you do not speak the language. Different networks, different apps. It is a normal feeling, and it fades fast — after two or three sessions, it becomes routine.

Public charging is not one system — it is a collection of networks, each running its own chargers. The skill is knowing how to find a charger, start a session, and move on without getting in anyone else's way.

The two kinds of public charger

The slower kind is found where cars sit for a while — shopping centres, workplaces, hotels, car parks. You plug in, go about your business, and come back to a car with more charge. It is built for time you were going to spend there anyway.

The faster kind sits along motorways and main roads, and at larger charging hubs. It adds charge much more quickly — the right choice when you are travelling and need to get moving again. Expect a short stop, long enough for a coffee and a stretch.

Networks, apps and finding a charger

There is no single company running every public charger. Different networks operate different sites, and most of them have an app that shows their chargers on a map, whether each one is free or in use, and how to start a session.

For a first-time driver, two or three apps cover most situations:

  • A map app that covers many networks. PlugShare lists chargers from lots of different operators in one place, with recent reports from other drivers about whether a charger is working.
  • The apps of the big networks near you. ChargePoint and Electrify America run large networks of their own, and their apps show their chargers' live status and let you start and pay for a session. Set up the apps for the networks on the routes you actually drive first.
  • Your car's own navigation. Many electric cars can plan a route that includes charging stops. It is worth learning how yours does this before a long trip, not during one.

Before you set off to a charger you have never used, check it in an app first. A charger that looks fine on a map may be out of service or occupied. A thirty-second check before you leave saves a detour when you arrive.

What a charging session actually looks like

Once you have done it once, the steps are always roughly the same:

  • Park properly. Pull into the marked bay so the cable reaches your car's charging port without stretching across the next bay.
  • Start the session. This is usually done in the network's app, by tapping a card on the charger, or on the charger's screen — read the instructions printed on the charger.
  • Plug in and check it started. Connect the cable, then look for the sign that charging has begun — a light on the charger, a message in the app, or the charging display in your car. If nothing happens, unplug and try again before assuming the charger is broken.
  • Wait, and keep an eye on it. Your app or car will show the charge level rising. Stay close enough to get back when the session ends.
  • Unplug and move on. When you have enough charge, stop the session in the app or on the charger, unplug the cable, return it to its holder, and move the car out of the bay. The charger is for charging, not parking.

Payment varies by network — some charge by energy used, some by time, and some by location or time of day. Check the price in the app before you start.

The etiquette that keeps it smooth

It works smoothly when everyone follows a few unwritten rules — all of them come down to leaving the charger ready for the next driver.

  • Move when you are done. The most common frustration at a public charger is a car sitting in a charging bay long after it has finished. Once your session is over, move the car and free the bay.
  • Do not unplug someone else's car. Unless a sign at the site says it is allowed, leave other people's sessions alone.
  • Do not park in a charging bay unless you are charging. A charging bay is not a convenient parking space. If you are not plugged in, park elsewhere.
  • Leave a note if something is wrong. If a charger is faulty, reporting it in the app helps the next driver avoid a wasted trip.
  • Be patient with first-timers. Everyone fumbled their first session — a quick offer of help costs nothing.

Where to look next

The short version

Public charging looks complicated from the outside and ordinary from the inside. Set up the apps for the networks on your routes, check a charger in the app before you drive to it, plug in, and move the car when you are done. Do that, and the charger becomes just another stop on the way.
