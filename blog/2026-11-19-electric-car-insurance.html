Electric car insurance: why quotes vary and what to check

Published November 19, 2026 · EV Forum

The first insurance quote is often the first surprise of buying an electric car. Two people can insure what looks like the same car and get quotes that are far apart. That is not a mistake — every insurer weighs the same facts differently, and electric cars give them a few extra things to weigh.

Why quotes vary so much

An insurer sets a price from its own picture of risk: how likely a claim is, and how much that claim would cost. Two insurers can look at the same driver and the same car and reach different answers, because they hold different claims data and weigh things differently. That is why comparing a handful of quotes matters more than any single "average" price.

So a quote you heard from a friend is only a rough hint. Their car, their history, where they park and how far they drive are all different from yours.

The car itself

An electric car gives an insurer three things to think about that a petrol car does not. The first is repair cost. When bodywork or a battery is damaged, the repair can need specialist parts, specialist training and a workshop approved by the maker. Longer waits mean a courtesy car for longer, and that feeds into the price.

The second is the battery. It is the most expensive single part of the car, and insurers handle it in different ways — some include it in the car's value, some treat it separately, and on a small number of older models the battery is leased rather than owned. That is worth checking before you buy, because a leased battery changes what you are actually insuring.

The third is performance. Many electric cars accelerate quickly and quietly, even modest family models, and insurers group cars by performance as well as price — so a car that feels calm to drive can still sit in a higher insurance group than a petrol car of the same size.

You, and where you live

Your own details move the quote as much as the car does: driving history, years licensed, previous claims and expected annual mileage all feed in. Where the car sleeps at night matters too — a locked garage, a driveway and a street are priced differently — and so does the area, since theft and accident rates vary from place to place. It is the same set of questions every driver answers, and it is why the same car can be cheap to insure for one person and costly for another.

The cover itself

Quotes only compare fairly when the cover is the same. A cheap quote with a high excess, no courtesy car and no windscreen cover is a different product from a pricier quote that includes them, so read what is included before you compare the numbers.

A few things are worth asking about on an electric car. Is the battery covered for accidental damage, and to its full value? Is the charging cable covered if it is stolen or damaged while plugged in away from home? Does the policy cover charging at a public charger, and what happens if the car runs out of charge — is recovery to a charger included? Those details are the ones that matter on the day something goes wrong.

Getting quotes you can compare

The most useful time to get quotes is before you buy the car, not after. Get quotes for the exact model and trim you are considering, using the same details for every quote: the same annual mileage, parking arrangements and excess.

Make sure the insurer knows it is an electric car. If it is listed under a petrol version of the same model by mistake, the quote is for the wrong car and the cover may not hold when you need it. Check the registration number pulls up the right model, and that the declared value matches what the car is worth.

Insurance rules and prices change, and they vary by place, so check the current rules for your area from an official source before you rely on anything in this guide.

A short checklist before you buy a policy

  • Compare like with like. Same cover, same excess, same details, or the prices mean nothing.
  • Ask about the battery. How it is covered, and whether a leased battery changes anything.
  • Ask about charging. Cable cover, public charging, and recovery if you run flat.
  • Quote before you buy the car. The insurance cost is part of the running cost, not an afterthought.
  • Check the details are right. Electric model, correct trim, correct value — a wrong listing can leave you uncovered.

The short version

Electric car insurance varies because insurers price repair costs, the battery and performance differently — and because your history, mileage and where you park matter just as much as the car. Get quotes before you buy, compare cover side by side, and ask specifically about the battery, the charging cable and recovery.
