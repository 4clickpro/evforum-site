Your first long trip in an electric car: planning it step by step

Published November 21, 2026 · EV Forum

A long trip is the moment most new electric car owners wonder about. Around town, the car is simple: charge at home, drive, repeat. A few hundred miles in a day is different — it asks you to think about where the energy comes from along the way. The good news is that a long trip is mostly planning, not worrying.

Start with the route, not the charger

Plot your route first, then work out where charging fits into it. Most navigation apps and the car's own system can suggest charging stops — but check them yourself before you set off.

The habit that makes trips calm is planning to charge before you need to, not when you are nearly empty. Aim to arrive at each stop with a comfortable margin left in the battery, so a closed or busy charger is an inconvenience rather than a problem. Pick stops where a break makes sense anyway — somewhere with food, a restroom and somewhere to stretch your legs.

Get the car ready the night before

Charge at home the night before so you leave with as full a battery as your car allows. If you are not sure what full means for your model — some makers suggest a lower daily limit for battery health — check the manual or the car's own settings rather than guessing.

Check tyre pressures, windscreen wash and any software updates while you are still at home. Make sure the charging cable is in the car, that you know which connector your car uses, and that the apps you need for public charging are installed and signed in. Sorting an account at a charger, in the rain, is the version of this trip nobody enjoys.

How charging stops actually work

Fast charging is not like filling a tank. A battery takes energy quickly when it is low and more slowly as it fills up, so the last part of a charge takes much longer than the first. On a trip, that usually means two shorter stops are quicker overall than one long one — charge enough to reach the next stop comfortably, then move on.

Stop more often on a first trip. Two relaxed stops beat one tense one.

Plan for the things that change range

Your range on a long trip will not match the figure in the brochure, and it does not need to. Speed is the biggest factor: steady motorway driving uses more energy than town driving. Cold weather, rain, hills and a loaded car all add their share.

The answer is not to drive slowly and anxiously — it is to plan your stops with real conditions in mind. Leave a margin for the weather and for a detour. If the trip is in winter, read our guide on winter driving in an electric car before you go.

When a charger does not work

Sometimes a charger is out of order, busy, or will not start a session. That is the main reason the plan includes a backup stop. Move on to the next charger rather than spending your margin trying to fix the first one.

If you are at a site with several chargers and one will not work, try another before giving up on the site. Do not let one bad charger set the tone for the trip — drivers who plan with a backup in mind usually remember the drive, not the charger that did not cooperate.

Charging where you stay

If your trip includes an overnight stay, check whether the place you are staying offers charging. Arriving with a low battery and leaving with a full one, without a special trip to a charger, is the easiest version of a long journey. Ask when you book — the listing may not mention it. If there is no charging where you stay, find a charger near your destination, or a fast charger on the road out the next morning.

A short checklist before you set off

  • Map the route and the stops. Know where each charging stop is, and have a backup for each one.
  • Leave with a full battery. Charge at home the night before.
  • Pack the cable and the apps. Cable in the car, apps signed in and payment methods ready.
  • Plan for real conditions. Speed, cold and hills all reduce range — leave a margin.
  • Stop before you must. Charge with room to spare, where a break is welcome.
  • Know where you will charge overnight. Ask your stay, or find a charger near your destination.

The short version

A long trip in an electric car comes down to three habits: plan your stops before you need them, always have a backup, and charge where a break is welcome anyway. Do that, and the trip feels like driving — because that is mostly what it is.
