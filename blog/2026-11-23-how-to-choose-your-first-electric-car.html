How to choose your first electric car: a shortlist that fits your week

Published November 23, 2026 · EV Forum

Choosing a first electric car is easy to overthink. There are dozens of models, each with its own figures, and every one of them looks like the right answer in a showroom. The trick is to start somewhere much simpler: your own week.

Start with your week, not the car

Before you look at a single model, write down a normal week: the commute, the school run, the weekly shop, the regular visit to family — and the occasional longer trip, honestly counted rather than imagined. Most weeks are far more regular than we think, and once you can see yours on paper, the choice narrows quickly. A car is bought for the journeys you make every week, not the one exceptional journey a year.

Work out your charging reality first

Charging is the one thing that changes most between electric cars and petrol cars, so sort it out before you fall for a particular model. If you have a driveway or a garage, home charging is usually the simplest setup — read our guide on home charging explained and ask a licensed electrician what your home can support. If you park on the street or in a shared car park, your plan will lean on public charging instead, and our guide to public charging without the stress explains how the networks and apps work.

Either way, be honest about it. A car is only convenient if charging it fits into your week without rearranging your life around it.

Shortlist by size and shape, not by badge

It is tempting to start with a brand you like and work outwards from there. It is usually more useful to start with size and shape. How many seats do you actually use, week in and week out? How much space does the weekly shop, the pushchair, the dog, or the work gear really need? Can the car fit where you park it at home and at work?

Visit a dealer and check the practical things: sit in the back seat yourself, load the boot with something the size of what you carry, and fold the seats down if that is something you do. Cars that look similar on paper can feel very different when you actually live with them.

Buy the range your week needs

Range is the figure everyone asks about first, and the one most often misunderstood. A range figure is a test result, not a promise — it changes with speed, weather, heating and driving style, as our guide on how to read a range figure explains. So do not buy the biggest range figure you can afford and call it safety. Buy the range that covers your normal week with a comfortable margin, plus your longest regular trip, and let the occasional very long journey be handled by a charging stop on the way. A car with far more range than your week needs is usually a bigger and more expensive car than you need, too.

Test drive with a purpose

Once you have a shortlist of three, drive them — all three, on the same kind of roads you actually use. A short loop around the dealer's block tells you very little. Take each car on your normal route if you can: the town streets, the faster road, the car park you use every day. Park it, reverse it, load it, and sit in it long enough to notice whether the seat suits you.

While you are there, ask the dealer the questions that matter to your week: how the car charges at home and in public, what the battery warranty covers and for how long, and what servicing looks like.

Check the costs around the car

The price on the windscreen is only part of what a car costs to own. Before you settle on one model, get insurance quotes for the cars on your shortlist — quotes can vary more than people expect, and our guide on electric car insurance explains why. Ask about tyres and servicing, and work out what charging will cost on your normal pattern rather than guessing. Incentives and tax rules change and vary by place, so check the current rules for your area from an official source.

A short checklist for your shortlist

  • Write down your week. The regular trips, honestly counted — that is what the car has to do.
  • Sort charging first. Home, public, or a mix — and make sure it fits your week.
  • Choose size and shape before brand. Seats, boot space, and whether it fits where you park.
  • Match range to your week. Your normal trips with a comfortable margin, not the biggest figure available.
  • Drive three cars on your own roads. Your route, your car park, your load.
  • Ask about the battery warranty in writing. Length, what it covers, and whether it transfers.
  • Price the whole picture. Insurance quotes, servicing, tyres, and charging on your normal pattern.

The short version

A good first electric car is not the one with the most impressive figures. It is the one that fits your week: your trips, your parking, your charging, and your budget once insurance and running costs are counted.
