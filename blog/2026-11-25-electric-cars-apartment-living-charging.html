Electric cars and apartment living: charging without a driveway

Published November 25, 2026 · EV Forum

Most electric car advice assumes you have a driveway and a charger on the wall at home. Plenty of people do not. If you live in an apartment, charging needs a plan instead of a plug by the front door. The question is whether charging fits your week, your building and your parking.

First, work out how much charging you actually need

Before you think about chargers, think about your week. How far do you drive on a normal day, and how far on your longest regular trip? Many people drive far less than they imagine, and a parked car has plenty of chances to charge — just not at home. A short commute is a very different charging problem from a long daily drive. Write down a typical week before you look at any car, because that week decides what kind of charging you need. And think in sessions, not full charges: topping up while you shop, or while the car sits for an hour somewhere, is how most apartment charging works.

Ask your building before you assume the answer is no

Check your own building first. Some apartment blocks already have shared chargers in the car park, or are planning them. Ask the landlord or building manager what exists, what is planned, and what the rules are. If there is nothing yet, ask what it would take. Rules about chargers in a shared car park vary a lot by place and by building, so check the current rules for your area from an official source and get any permission in writing before you spend money.

Even if a charger for your own space is not possible, a shared one can be — a charger several residents take turns using is a different conversation from one person asking alone. If other residents are interested, ask together. And if your building says no for now, that is useful information too: it tells you to plan around public charging instead.

Charging near home, not at home

If home charging is off the table, the next question is what sits near where you already go. Look at the chargers around your home, your workplace and the places you visit every week — supermarkets, shopping centres, gyms and car parks. A charger you pass anyway is worth far more than a faster one across town. Check whether the chargers near you are usually free or usually busy, and at what times. The same goes for workplace charging: if your employer has chargers, a car that charges while you work solves the apartment problem entirely. Read our guide on public charging without the stress for how the networks and apps work, so you know what a session looks like before your first one.

Build a routine that fits your week

Apartment charging works as a habit, not a hunt. Pick one or two regular charging spots and fold charging into something you already do — charge while you shop, while you work, or on the way home from something, not as a trip of its own. The goal is a routine you barely notice.

Keep a buffer in the battery and a backup charger in mind, so one closed or occupied charger does not leave you stuck. The routine does not have to be perfect; it just has to work on a bad week as well as a good one.

What to check before you buy

Do not buy the car and then work out the charging. Try the routine first, while you still have your current car. Spend a week or two using the chargers near your home and your workplace, and see whether they are free when you need them. If the routine feels easy before you own the car, it will feel easy afterwards. If it feels like a chore, believe that.

Also check the practical details: whether your parking space is yours or first-come-first-served, and whether your building has rules about cables across shared areas — trailing a cable across a walkway is usually not allowed. The EV Forum directory lists charging networks and dealers near you.

A short checklist for apartment living

  • Map your week first: how far you drive, and where the car sits for an hour or more.
  • Ask your building: shared chargers, plans, rules — and get any permission in writing.
  • Check chargers near home and work: how busy they are, and when.
  • Ask your employer about workplace charging.
  • Try the routine before you buy: a week or two of real sessions, not a guess.
  • Keep a backup charger in mind and a buffer in the battery.
  • Check the parking rules — your space, cables, and shared areas.

Where to look next

The short version

No driveway does not mean no electric car. It means charging happens where your week already takes you — at work, at the shops, at a charger near home — instead of on your own wall. Ask your building first, try the routine before you buy, and build a habit that works on a bad week as well as a good one. If the routine feels easy, the car will too.
