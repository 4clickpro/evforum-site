Buying a used EV — what do you check before you sign?

EV Forum Team · Started November 16, 2026 · Opening post — no replies yet

A used electric car can be one of the smartest buys on the road — or one of the most expensive mistakes. The difference is rarely the badge or the mileage on the clock. It is what you check before you sign. Buying a used EV is not like buying a used petrol car: there is no engine to listen to, but there is a battery, and the battery is the part of the car you cannot see, cannot hear, and would cost the most to replace. So the checks are different, and they matter more than the haggle.

Start with the battery. Ask the seller for a battery health report — a proper state-of-health reading. Ask how the car was mostly charged: at home on a slow charger, or on rapid chargers? Check the car's original range on the manufacturer's own page, then look at what the car reports at a full charge and ask whether the gap makes sense for your journeys. If the seller cannot or will not show you a battery report, treat that as information in itself.

Then check the car like any used car, because the rest of it still wears out. Tyres on a heavy car with instant torque can wear faster than you expect, so look at the tread and ask when they were last changed. Brakes last longer on an EV, but they still need to work — listen on the test drive and check the service history. Look at the charging port and cable, try plugging in at a public charger during the test drive if you can, and make sure the software is up to date. A service history from a dealer or EV specialist counts for more than receipts from a garage that never touched an EV.

Finally, the paperwork. Check the ownership history and whether any finance is still outstanding on the car. Find out what warranty is left — the battery warranty is often separate from the rest of the car, and the terms come from the manufacturer, so check them directly rather than taking a seller's word for it. If the price is serious money, an independent EV inspection is cheap insurance. None of this is about finding a perfect car. It is about knowing what you are buying, so the car you drive home is the car you thought you bought.
