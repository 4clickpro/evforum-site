What does running an EV actually cost you month to month?

EV Forum Team · Started November 17, 2026 · Opening post — no replies yet

Ask an EV owner what their car costs to run and you will get two answers at once: "hardly anything" and "it depends". Both are true, and that is exactly why the question is worth asking properly. The headline comparison — cheap electricity against petrol — is only one line in the monthly bill. The real figure is a bundle: charging, insurance, servicing, tyres, and the odd cost nobody puts in a brochure. Add them up for one driver and the EV wins easily. Add them up for another and the gap is much smaller than the adverts suggest.

Charging is the biggest line, and the most variable. A driver who charges at home overnight, ideally on an off-peak tariff, usually sees the lowest bills — that is where the famous savings come from. A driver who relies on public chargers, especially rapid ones, pays noticeably more per mile, and the price varies by network and by time of day. The honest way to judge it is to track one real month: how much electricity went into the car, what you paid for it, and how many miles you drove. A single cheap charge tells you nothing; a month of them tells you almost everything.

Then come the quieter lines. Insurance can be higher for some electric cars, so it is worth comparing quotes rather than assuming. Servicing is usually simpler — fewer moving parts, no oil changes — but tyres can wear faster on a heavy car with instant torque, and a set of tyres is not cheap. Tax and registration depend on where you live, so check the local rules rather than a headline from another country. And depreciation is not a monthly bill, but it shapes the real cost of the car over the years you own it.

So is an EV cheaper to run month to month? For many drivers, yes — clearly. For others, especially those without home charging, the answer is closer than they expected. That is not a reason to avoid one; it is a reason to go in with your eyes open. Tell us what your month actually looks like.

  2. Do you charge mostly at home or mostly in public, and how much difference does it make to your bill?
  3. What cost surprised you — the one nobody warned you about?
  4. Servicing and tyres: cheaper than you expected, or not?
  5. If someone asked you "is it actually cheaper to run?" — what would your honest answer be?
