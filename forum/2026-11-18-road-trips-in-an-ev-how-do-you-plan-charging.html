Road trips in an EV — how do you plan charging on a long drive?

EV Forum Team · Started November 18, 2026 · Opening post — no replies yet

The first long drive in an electric car changes how you think about a journey. In a petrol car, a road trip is simple: drive until the tank is low, stop for five minutes, carry on. In an EV, the fuel stop becomes part of the plan — where you charge, how long you stop, and what you do while you wait. That sounds like a hassle until you have done it once, and then most drivers find it is a different kind of trip, not a worse one.

The planning starts before you leave. Most drivers pick their charging stops in advance, using an app or the car's own navigation, and aim to arrive with a comfortable buffer rather than running the battery down to empty. The stops that work best are the ones that fit the journey: a charger near a place you would stop anyway — somewhere to eat, stretch your legs, let the kids out. A stop that feels like a break is a good stop; a stop that feels like waiting is a bad one. And the route matters too: motorway chargers are busier at holiday weekends, so experienced drivers build in a backup stop, just in case the first one is full or out of order.

There are honest trade-offs. Charging takes longer than filling a tank, so a long drive takes longer overall — though many drivers say the forced breaks leave them less tired at the other end. Winter, hills, and motorway speed all affect how far a charge takes you, so the number on the dashboard is a guide, not a promise. And public charging costs more than charging at home, so a road trip is usually the most expensive driving an EV owner does. None of that makes road trips impractical; it makes them a skill, and like any skill, the second one is easier than the first.

The best advice usually comes from drivers who have actually done it — the routes they trust, the stops they swear by, and the mistakes they only made once. Tell us how you plan a long drive.
