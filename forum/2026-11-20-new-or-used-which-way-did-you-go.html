New or used — which way did you go, and would you do it again?

EV Forum Team · Started November 20, 2026 · Opening post — no replies yet

Almost every EV buyer reaches the same fork in the road before anything else: buy new, or buy used? A new car brings the latest tech, a full warranty, and the simple fact that you know its whole history — because it has none yet. But it also costs the most, and a new car loses value fastest in its first year or two. A used EV costs far less up front, and someone else has already taken that first hit on value. The trade-off is a shorter remaining warranty, older charging tech, and a battery with some miles already on it.

The case for new is peace of mind. You choose the exact model, colour and options, and the warranty covers the car — including the battery — for years to come. If you plan to keep the car a long time, that certainty can be worth the price. The case for used is value: the same money buys a bigger, better-equipped car, or the car you want for much less. The trade-off is homework. You need to check the battery health, the service history, how the car was charged, and what warranty is left — and be honest about whether you can live with an older screen, slower charging, or fewer driver-assistance features.

There is also the middle ground people forget about: nearly-new cars, ex-demonstrators and former lease cars that are a year or two old, with most of their warranty left and a much lower price than new. Some buyers go new and keep the car for a decade. Others buy used, run it for two or three years, and move on. Both approaches work — what matters is going in with your eyes open, knowing what you are paying for and what you are giving up. And the best guide is not a review or a brochure; it is the drivers who have already made the choice and lived with it.

So tell us: did you buy new or used — and looking back, would you make the same choice again? What tipped the decision for you, and what would you tell someone standing at the same fork today?
