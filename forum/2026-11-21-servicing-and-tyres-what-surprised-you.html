Servicing and tyres — what surprised you about looking after an EV?

EV Forum Team · Started November 21, 2026 · Opening post — no replies yet

One of the first things people hear about electric cars is that there is less to look after — no oil to change, no exhaust, and far fewer moving parts than a petrol or diesel car. For many owners that turns out to be broadly true: the service schedule is lighter, and there are simply fewer routine jobs to do. But less to look after is not the same as nothing to look after, and the things that do need attention are often different from what drivers coming from a petrol or diesel car expect.

Servicing is the first surprise. The visits can be shorter and the checklist can look thin, yet the car still needs its checks: brakes, cabin filters, and the cooling system for the battery where the manufacturer specifies it, plus software updates. Regenerative braking means the friction brakes often last longer, but they still need inspecting — especially if they rarely get worked hard. And finding the right garage matters: not every independent workshop works on electric cars yet, while dealer servicing can feel expensive for what looks like a short list of jobs. Fewer routine jobs, but the jobs that remain need the right skills and the right kit.

Tyres are the second surprise, and for many owners the bigger one. Electric cars are heavy, and they deliver their power instantly, so tyres can wear faster than drivers expect — particularly on the driven axle. Some owners find the difference small and mostly down to driving style; others go through a set noticeably sooner than they did on their old petrol car. Either way, tyres become a larger share of the running costs than most people budget for, and the choice matters: the right tyres for the weight and the instant torque, rotated regularly and kept at the right pressure.

So what surprised you about looking after your EV — the servicing, the tyres, or a job you did not know the car still needed? Tell us what caught you out, what it cost you in time or money, and what you would warn a new owner about before they sign up for their first year of ownership.
