Charging without a driveway — how do you make it work?

EV Forum Team · Started November 22, 2026 · Opening post — no replies yet

Much of the advice about living with an electric car assumes you have a driveway and a charger on the wall — plug in at night, wake up to a full battery, and never think about it again. But plenty of drivers do not have a driveway at all. Flats, terraces, rented homes, and streets where parking is wherever you can find a space: for a lot of people, home charging is not an option, and the question is whether an electric car still works for them. The honest answer is that it can — but it takes more thought than the driveway version, and the routine looks different.

The options are real, just spread out. There are on-street chargers near home, chargers at work, chargers in supermarket and shopping car parks, and rapid chargers for a quicker top-up on the way home. Some drivers charge once or twice a week and treat it a bit like a petrol stop; others top up whenever they park somewhere with a charger, so the battery is rarely low. The trade-offs are honest ones: public charging usually costs more per unit than home electricity, a charger can be busy or out of order when you need it, and you need a backup plan for the evening your usual one is taken.

It also changes how you think about parking. Instead of parking anywhere, you start parking where there is a charger — or planning your week around one charging stop. A charging cable lives in the boot, and apps and maps become part of the routine: checking which chargers are free, which ones take contactless payment, and which ones need a membership. Some drivers have talked to their landlord or building manager about a charger, or found a kerbside charger on their street. Others simply make a weekly rapid charge part of the routine and get on with it.

So if you charge without a driveway, how do you actually make it work? What does your week look like, what surprised you about the cost or the convenience, and what would you say to someone in a flat who is wondering whether an electric car is realistic for them? Tell us what works, what does not, and what you wish you had known before you made the switch.
