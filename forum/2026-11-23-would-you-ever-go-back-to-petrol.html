Would you ever go back to a petrol car — and what would it take?

EV Forum Team · Started November 23, 2026 · Opening post — no replies yet

After a year or two in an electric car, most drivers get asked the same question: would you ever go back to petrol? It sounds simple, but the answer is rarely simple, because going back is not really about one thing. It is about how a car fits into your whole life — how it charges, how it drives, what it costs to run, and what your daily routine looks like now. Some say they would never go back, full stop. Others are more honest: they love the electric car, but there are days — a long trip with a queue at the chargers, a cold morning with a low battery — when the old way looks tempting.

The trade-off is worth spelling out honestly. An electric car is quiet, quick off the mark, and cheap to run when you can charge at home. But the petrol car had its own kind of freedom: fill up anywhere in five minutes, no app, no planning. For some drivers, the smoothness and low running costs of the EV outweigh all of that. For others, the convenience of petrol still wins on the days that matter most, like a holiday drive or a week with no home charging. And some drivers sit in the middle: they would happily keep the EV as the everyday car but want a petrol car for the long trips.

Then there is what would actually make someone switch back. Would it be one bad charging experience, a change in circumstances — moving to a flat with no charger, for example — or simply the price of the next car? Or is it more about what comes next: a cheaper EV, a longer-range one, or a charging network that makes the whole question disappear? The answer probably says as much about where someone lives and how they drive as it does about the cars themselves.

So we want to hear from people who have actually lived with an electric car for a while. If you switched from petrol, what do you miss, if anything? What would have to change for you to go back? And if you did go back — or you are thinking about it — what tipped the balance? Tell us what works, what does not, and what you would say to someone who is still deciding.
