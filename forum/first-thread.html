Home charging vs public charging — what works for you?

EV Forum Team · Started November 14, 2026 · Opening post — no replies yet

Ask ten electric car drivers where they charge, and you will get ten different answers. Some plug in at home every night and barely think about it. Others have no driveway, no garage, and no home charger at all, so the public network is their fuel station. Both are real ways to run an EV, and the honest answer to “which is better?” is usually: it depends on where you live, how far you drive, and what your week looks like.

Home charging wins on convenience for the people who can do it. You come home, you plug in, and the car is ready in the morning. There is no detour, no queue, and no standing around in a car park. Charging becomes background time — the car charges while you sleep. The trade-off is the setup: you need somewhere to park off the street, and getting a charger fitted takes planning, permission where you rent, and a bit of patience.

Public charging wins on flexibility. Fast chargers on main routes make long trips possible, and chargers at shops, workplaces, and car parks let you top up while you are already doing something else. The trade-off is that it takes more thought. You check where the chargers are, whether they are working, whether someone else is using them, and how long you will actually be there. A charger that is broken or busy can turn a simple trip into a frustrating one — the part nobody mentions until it happens to them.

Then there is the middle ground, where most drivers end up. A bit of home charging, a bit of public charging, a workplace charger if you are lucky, and a plan B for the days the plan does not work. The happiest drivers are not the ones with the perfect setup. They are the ones whose setup matches their life: short commutes and a home charger, or no home charger but a reliable public charger near home or work.

So this first thread is simple, and we want real answers. Not theory, not what a brochure says — what actually works for you, week in and week out. If you are thinking of buying your first EV, read on. If you already drive one, tell us what you wish someone had told you before you bought it.

Over to you

  1. Where do you charge most of the time — at home, at work, or in public — and why did it end up that way?
  2. If you charge at home, what do you wish you had known before the charger went in?
  3. If you cannot charge at home, how do you make it work — and what would make it easier?
  4. What is your plan B when your usual charger is busy or out of order?
  5. For anyone still deciding: what is the one charging question you would ask an EV driver before buying?
